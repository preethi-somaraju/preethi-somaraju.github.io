Preethi Somaraju

Data engineer — Houston, Texas

For about ten years I have worked on the unglamorous half of data: getting it out of the systems that produce it, into a shape someone can actually use, reliably enough that nobody has to think about it. That started with hardware test telemetry at Qualcomm, where I learned that data arrives messy and on its own schedule.

At the University of North Texas I built a cloud ingestion pipeline on Azure — Data Factory, Blob Storage, Azure SQL — that replaced a manual collection process, while finishing an MS in Data Science. After that came four years of consulting: ETL automation across client databases, forecasting work, and the reporting layers that made any of it useful to the people paying for it.

Now I am rebuilding that skill set on AWS, in the open. One lab a week for a year — from an empty account through Glue, Athena, Iceberg, Kinesis, Redshift and Airflow, to a lakehouse at the end. Terraform for everything, torn down after each one.

Every lab records the tests that passed and the ones meant to fail, with real command output. A successful terraform apply is not evidence that anything works.

The first is account foundations — on why a budget alarm should exist before any other resource does, and what happens when you ask S3 to make a bucket public while Block Public Access is switched on. The rest follow weekly, and the code is all public.

Older work sits alongside it: an analytics pipeline over NYC taxi data using S3, Glue and Athena, a cloud telemetry ETL pipeline, and demand forecasting on daily retail sales.

I am looking for data engineering work in Houston or remote, and I am authorised to work in the US.
