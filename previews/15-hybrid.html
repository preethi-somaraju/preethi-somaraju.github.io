Data engineer — Houston, Texas

For about ten years I have worked on the unglamorous half of data: getting it out of the systems that produce it, into a shape someone can actually use, reliably enough that nobody has to think about it. That started with hardware test telemetry at Qualcomm, where I learned that data arrives messy and on its own schedule.

Since then: a cloud ingestion pipeline at the University of North Texas, four years of consulting on ETL and forecasting, and now a year of rebuilding that skill set on AWS in the open.

Now

One AWS lab a week, for a year

Fifty-two labs, from an empty account through Glue, Athena, Iceberg, Kinesis, Redshift and Airflow, ending at a lakehouse. Terraform for everything, torn down after each one.

Every lab records the tests that passed and the ones meant to fail, with real command output. A successful terraform apply is not evidence that anything works.

The first is account foundations — on why a budget alarm should exist before any other resource does, and what happens when you ask S3 to make a bucket public while Block Public Access is switched on. The code is public, and the rest follow weekly.
